# Open Current review, 18 November 2026

Open Current served usable forecasts and passed its regression suites. The largest live issues were undetected delays in European collection, unequal samples behind the wind-model comparison, and incorrect time handling in two charts. A separate recovery drill exposed a way to lose issued forecasts after a stale archive restore.

The measurements below use one Pages deployment, prepared at 11:12 UTC and published at 11:13 UTC on 18 November. The watchdog observation is from 11:56 UTC. A direct provider check followed at 12:21 UTC. The review covered the source at `8f83e96`, the deployed dashboard, scheduled workflows, collector, forecast serving and experiments. Failure drills used local copies or browser request interception. [Reviewed revision](https://github.com/HuayuanSong/open-current/tree/8f83e960230bac74090946f02136c662712e3d4a) · [Pages run](https://github.com/HuayuanSong/open-current/actions/runs/44292468898)

## Data availability and recovery

**High · A healthy watchdog can hide hours of missing European updates.** At 11:56 UTC the Danish snapshot was 44 minutes old. Germany's latest power reading was 9 hours 27 minutes old, the median across the available European country feeds was 9 hours 57 minutes, and the last imported European collection had finished at 04:53 UTC. Since the collector split on 16 November, 117 checks in the retained history saw a median power age above six hours. All 117 returned no alert reason. The watchdog records European ages but bases source alerts on Energinet and only dispatches the Pages workflow. It also ignores the separate public wind issue time. Replaying the same health payload with a wind forecast over 20 hours old still returned no alert. [Watchdog assessment](../watchdog/src/check.mjs#L74) · [Recovery dispatch](../watchdog/src/check.mjs#L174) · [European collection](https://github.com/HuayuanSong/open-current/actions/runs/44238530760)

At 12:21 UTC I queried the German source and the live site together. Energy-Charts already had a usable 10:45 generation reading and a complete 09:45 mix, while Open Current still showed 02:30. In the 24 hours through 12:03 UTC, only three European collector runs started. The publisher started 21 times, 18 by dispatch and three by schedule, with a median of 75 minutes between starts. Show the collection and wind issue times independently. Give the watchdog bounded recovery for an overdue collector. If collection succeeded but the provider still serves old readings, show the source delay. [German provider query](https://api.energy-charts.info/v2/public_power?country=de&start=2026-11-18T00%3A21Z&end=2026-11-18T12%3A21Z) · [Collector schedule](../.github/workflows/collect-europe.yml) · [Published health fields](../scripts/health.py#L19)

**High · The recovery schedule repeatedly misses the wind issuance window.** In the same 24 hours, four successful publisher runs started at 11 minutes past the hour. All four logged `waiting-for-source-hour` around 13 minutes past and issued no public wind forecast. The model correctly waits 15 minutes for observations and rejects a forecast whose first target has already ended. The watchdog sees a fresh publication and can wait over an hour to try again. Schedule a wind attempt after the source buffer and expose the reason for a skipped issue. Keep the safeguard against retrospective issuance. [Watchdog schedule](../watchdog/cloudflare.config.ts#L15) · [Source buffer](../forecast/data.py#L260) · [Wind issuance rule](../forecast/fresh_wind.py#L85) · [Example successful run](https://github.com/HuayuanSong/open-current/actions/runs/44292468898)

**High · Deployment verification can accept an older forecast archive.** The publication check hashes four public JSON products. The compressed archive that seeds the next run is outside that check. On a fresh checkout, restore compares its timestamp with the repository's October seed. Substituting the previous day's real archive left today's verification manifest identical and restore accepted it. A subsequent publication based on that copy could drop **21 baseline and 16 public wind issues**, along with research evidence. The two actual snapshots retained all their shared records. Include the archive digest in publication verification and verify restore against an independent last-successful checkpoint. Keep a recoverable copy and reject unexplained rollbacks before writing. [Publication manifest](../scripts/verify_publication.py#L14) · [Archive restore](../forecast/data.py#L88)

**Medium · One unavailable feed can disable independent features.** With European generation older than seven days and a valid five-minute-old Danish reading, normalization wrote Danish data and then stopped the entire build before publishing it. On first page load, blocking only `denmark.json` left the European map empty and prevented the explorer from retrying automatically. A separate forecast drill showed that losing all usable DMI runs also blocks an East Denmark wind issue, although East uses a zero DMI weight. Publish and initialize available feeds independently, with per-region issue times and last-good records. [Normalization guard](../scripts/normalize.py#L369) · [Browser startup](../dist/app.js#L1085) · [Weather source selection](../forecast/fresh_wind.py#L100)

## What the dashboard shows

**High · The wind-model selector can reverse the apparent ranking.** Each model's “Live mean error” uses its own last seven days of issues. Switching models in “Forecast vs. actual” also selects an older issue independently for each model. In this deployment those comparison records even used different reference hours, 08:00 and 09:00 UTC on 17 November. The snapshot supports the following calculations of mean absolute error in megawatts. Lower is better. [Comparison selection](../forecast/publish.py#L137) · [Score displayed](../dist/forecasts.js#L144)

| Evaluation sample | West, previous | West, current | East, previous | East, current |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Dashboard, 4,237 previous and 630 current forecast-hours per area | 954.3 | 1,026.1 | 168.8 | 129.6 |
| Matched, 579 forecast-hours per model and area | 1,270.6 | 1,037.6 | 215.3 | 130.2 |

The matched calculation took the first current-version issue per reference hour and the latest previous-model issue available by that issue time. It used the same target hour, lead and Energinet observation, excluded targets already ended at issuance, and applied the published 15-minute observation buffer. The 26 common reference hours covered **44 distinct observed hours**, with no fully observed 48-hour forecast. The median issue-time difference was 1.3 minutes and the maximum was 10.8 minutes. This sample favors the current model in both areas. Report matched errors by region and lead alongside each model's availability, and accumulate evidence across more days and wind conditions before making a model decision. [Scoring eligibility](../forecast/publish.py#L33)

**Medium · An already-ended hour is labeled as a forecast.** In 69 of 280 archived baseline issues the first target hour had ended before issuance. For example, East Denmark's 11:12 UTC demand issue displayed a forecast of 2,301.75 MW for the hour ending at 11:00, alongside the observed 2,305.02 MW. The chart, average and peak include that value. The published error calculation correctly excludes it. Filter elapsed targets in the chart and summary, label the number of forecast hours actually shown, and preserve the original issue in the archive. [Chart and summary](../dist/forecast-model.js#L68) · [Issuance handling](../forecast/run.py#L90)

**Medium · Historical prices can show the next tariff early.** In West Denmark on 13 November, hovering at 20:56:47 UTC showed €209.4/MWh. The price in force from 20:45 to 21:00 was **€111.81/MWh**. The next interval was €209.35/MWh. The chart draws a sloped line and snaps the tooltip to the nearest sample. Use the existing interval-aware price lookup for a stepped line and tooltip, preserve gaps after an interval expires, and expose interval inspection to keyboard and touch users. The existing historical tooltip listens for mouse movement. [Chart and tooltip](../dist/app.js#L807) · [Interval-aware price lookup](../dist/model.js#L65)

**Forecast reliability · The previous model's prediction bands miss their 80% target.** Seven-day observed coverage for the previous wind model was **38.2% in the west and 44.0% in the east**. Demand reached 50.8% and 62.2%, and daylight solar reached 72.4% and 75.3%. Repeat forecasts for the same weather count toward these figures. The dashboard already warns about undercoverage. Give measured coverage prominence wherever a band is shown. Keep the current wind model's range withheld pending prospective calibration by region and lead. [Range scoring](../forecast/publish.py#L61) · [Range display](../dist/forecasts.js#L171)

## Model validation and experiments

**High for the December decision · The original weather trial can no longer meet its frozen protocol.** As of this snapshot, 22 of 42 daily issue windows had elapsed. Eight issued and 14 were missed. Even a perfect remaining run yields **28/42, or 66.7%**. The 80% rule requires at least **34**. The status still says “collecting.” Continue preserving the observations and reporting descriptive errors. Mark the coverage criterion as unreachable now and record the outcome at the December review. This protocol belongs to the October multi-target weather trial. The newer public wind comparison has its own evidence. [Frozen protocol](../forecast/trial/protocol.json#L18) · [Decision calculation](../forecast/trial/evaluate.py#L58) · [Published trial summary](https://huayuansong.github.io/open-current/data/forecast-trial.json)

**Medium · The internal short-term wind challenger treats missing readings as observed.** Missingness is encoded as zero and presence as one. Training preparation and live research issuance both check whether the mask values are finite, which accepts zero. The 17 November 05:16 run saved one missing wind hour in each region and issued challenger predictions. Twelve research issues spanning that missing hour bypassed the intended fallback. Historical preparation retained 26 of 1,003 reference hours with incomplete recent wind. Check explicitly for observed values, test the actual mask representation and version a corrected research run before using it for promotion. This issue is in the experimental 1–6 hour challenger. [Live check](../forecast/wind_refinement.py#L103) · [Training check](../research/forecasting/wind_refinement/prepare.py#L43) · [Saved run](https://github.com/HuayuanSong/open-current/actions/runs/44022802306)

**Medium · Production weather validation can silently accept the wrong region or units.** Using a real saved ECMWF response, the validator accepted both an East/West location swap and wind speeds converted from m/s to km/h with matching changed unit metadata. The genuine sample had the expected units and locations. Validate coordinates within grid tolerance, units and UTC offset before accepting or caching inputs. The ensemble-research code already performs these checks for its variables. [Production validator](../forecast/weather_runs.py#L50) · [Regional assignment](../forecast/weather_sources.py#L111) · [Existing validation example](../forecast/weather_ensemble.py#L28)

**Medium · Two experimental ledgers can give old observations a newer capture time.** Both the October weather trial and the wind-update trial use processing time when freezing retained observations. A reading retrieved at age 23h50m can pass a 24-hour minimum when processed at age 24h07m after a failed refresh. Both functions reproduced the acceptance locally. Store retrieval time with each refreshed reading and use that time for eligibility. Existing records lack the provenance needed to count affected issues reliably. [October ledger](../forecast/trial/ledger.py#L66) · [Wind ledger](../forecast/wind_update.py#L63) · [Previous reproduction](code-review-2026-11-01.md#deferred-finding-trial-observation-provenance)

## Verification and scope

- All **147 Python**, **20 dashboard JavaScript** and **18 watchdog** tests passed. Live browser journeys passed at 390, 768, 1024 and 1440 pixels, plus 15 fixture-backed browser regressions. The main journeys produced no JavaScript errors or third-party browser requests.
- Across 34 configured countries, 41,986 generation, exchange and price rows passed checks on timestamp order, uniqueness and agreement with the index. Checks also covered share bounds and exchange arithmetic. There were 33 countries with a generation reading and 29 with a complete mix. These are structural checks on the downloaded data.
- All 280 baseline and 29 public wind issues contained 48 consecutive hourly targets and finite, nonnegative point predictions. Saved bounds were ordered where present. All 29 public wind record hashes verified. Baseline and October trial CPU outputs matched their release fixtures within tolerance. The wind refit matched exactly, and the demand explanation check passed all five tested leads in each region. These checks establish serving consistency. Forecast accuracy requires subsequently observed outcomes.
- Three cold mobile browser runs at 390 pixels, 4× CPU slowdown, 1.6 Mbit/s and 150 ms latency had a median largest contentful paint of **0.56 seconds**, while the map's country shapes appeared at **4.04 seconds**. The desktop reference drew them at 0.67 seconds. Mobile subresources transferred about 705 kB, including 536 kB of data and geography. Loading the map independently from the Danish detail feed would also address the startup failure above. These figures are lab measurements on one connection. [Startup dependencies](../dist/app.js#L1085)

Fix the freshness watchdog and archive recovery first. Correct chart time handling and expose a paired wind comparison before using the dashboard to choose a model. Preserve the original issues and protocols when correcting experimental evidence.
